# AI Security & Resilience Assessment

A vendor-agnostic assessment for organizations that need to understand how prepared they are to secure a business that increasingly depends on artificial intelligence.

> **The goal is not simply to secure AI. The goal is to secure the business after AI becomes part of how the business operates.**

## What this project evaluates

The assessment separates four primary dimensions:

1. **AI Security Maturity** — how consistently governance, controls, monitoring, validation and response capabilities are implemented.
2. **AI Exposure** — how much business risk is created by AI adoption, sensitive data, external exposure, criticality, privilege and third-party dependencies.
3. **AI Autonomy** — how much authority AI has to recommend, initiate or independently execute actions.
4. **Business Resilience** — how prepared the organization is for AI compromise, outage, degradation, provider disruption or unexpected behavior.

A supporting **Accountability Coverage** indicator evaluates ownership, human sponsorship, residual-risk acceptance and decision rights.

## Assessment journey

`Discover → Classify → Govern → Threat Model → Design & Build Securely → Protect → Validate → Monitor → Respond & Recover → Measure & Improve`

## Core principles

- Protect the business, not only the model.
- AI security is broader than LLM security.
- Models generate; agents act.
- Maturity and exposure are different.
- A weak control is not automatically the highest priority.
- Critical AI systems require explicit ownership and decision rights.
- Autonomous or semi-autonomous agents require accountable human sponsorship.
- Third-party AI is part of the enterprise AI estate.
- AI security includes prevention, detection, response and resilience.
- Recommendations describe capabilities and outcomes, not vendor products.

## Scoring philosophy

The scoring model is deterministic and explainable. Numerical scores are not generated by a large language model.

The assessment calculates:

- AI Security Maturity: 0–100
- AI Exposure: 0–100
- AI Autonomy: 0–5
- Business Resilience: 0–100
- Accountability Coverage: 0–100 (supporting indicator)

Recommendations are prioritized conceptually using:

`Control Gap × Exposure × Business Impact × Autonomy Modifier`

The exact implementation is documented in [`METHODOLOGY.md`](METHODOLOGY.md) and will evolve as the model is validated.

## Framework foundation

The methodology is informed by established public frameworks and standards, including:

- NIST AI Risk Management Framework
- NIST Generative AI Profile
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework
- ISO/IEC 42001
- ISO/IEC 27001
- OWASP GenAI Security guidance
- OWASP Agentic Security guidance
- MITRE ATLAS
- Cloud Security Alliance AI Controls Matrix

The project does not replace these frameworks. It translates their principles into an operational assessment and prioritized roadmap.

Framework alignment is an estimate of maturity coverage and **does not constitute certification, legal advice, regulatory advice, a formal audit or compliance validation**.

## Privacy principle

The assessment should not require confidential infrastructure data, credentials, API keys, internal hostnames or detailed incident information. It evaluates organizational capability and maturity rather than collecting sensitive technical configuration.
