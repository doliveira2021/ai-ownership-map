# AI Accountability & Decision Rights

## Purpose

AI governance must define not only policies but also who is accountable for outcomes and who has authority to make critical decisions.

The model does not enforce one universal organizational RACI. It evaluates whether responsibilities and decision rights are explicit, understood, auditable and maintained over time.

## Core principles

- No critical AI system should exist without an accountable business owner.
- No autonomous or semi-autonomous production agent should operate without an accountable human sponsor or owner.
- Every AI identity should have an owner.
- Increased autonomy, privilege or data access should have an explicit approval authority.
- High-risk AI deployments should have documented residual-risk acceptance.
- Incident authority must include the ability to contain, disable and restore AI safely.

## Accountability areas

The assessment should consider ownership for:

- AI use-case approval
- business outcomes
- AI risk classification
- security requirements
- data access
- model approval and changes
- architecture approval
- agent sponsorship
- agent privilege and autonomy
- tools/APIs
- third-party AI
- production operations
- monitoring
- incident response
- recovery
- residual-risk acceptance
- regulatory obligations
- decommissioning

## Agent accountability roles

For agents, distinguish where useful between:

- **Technical Identity** — identity used to authenticate and access systems
- **Business Owner** — accountable for the business outcome
- **Human Sponsor** — accountable for approved purpose, scope and operation
- **Security Owner** — accountable for security requirements and monitoring

These roles may overlap.

## Decision rights

Explicit decision rights should cover material changes such as:

- introducing a new AI system
- increasing data access
- connecting a new tool or API
- enabling autonomous actions
- changing models
- modifying critical system instructions
- adding agents
- increasing agent privileges
- enabling agent-to-agent delegation
- accepting residual risk
- disabling AI during an incident
- restoring AI after containment

## Accountability Coverage

The supporting Accountability Coverage score (0–100) should summarize whether critical ownership areas are clearly assigned. It should remain secondary to the four headline scores.

## RACI use

A RACI-style matrix may be generated as an illustrative target-state model. It must be labeled as guidance rather than a compliance requirement.
